## Running it with people

When the critique is a session rather than a solo pass:

- **State the constraints first**, before showing anything: what is fixed, what is being asked
  about, what stage this is at. A reviewer who does not know the constraints will critique them.
- **Ask for findings against rules**, not for opinions. "Which of our stated rules does this
  violate?" produces different output from "what do you think?".
- **The designer does not defend.** They capture. Defence converts a critique into a negotiation
  and the findings stop arriving.
- **Separate the observation from the prescription.** "I could not tell which of these was the
  main action" is data. "Make it blue" is a solution to an unstated problem. Capture the first
  and treat the second as a hypothesis about the first.
- **Silence is not agreement.** Ask the quietest person directly (this is the same discipline
  `gate-review` applies to quiet gates).
- **Timebox per lens.** Unbounded critique drifts to whatever is most fun to discuss, which is
  never state completeness.
