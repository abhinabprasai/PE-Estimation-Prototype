Part 3The phases, step by step

What actually happens in each phase: the sequence, who owns what, what is produced, and what has to be true before the next thing starts. Read the phase you are in; skim the rest.

3.1 Phase 0: Intake PHASE 0

Owner: discovery-lead  ·  Command: /new-project then /intake-spec

A real phase, not a formality, and the only step that is never skipped, including when a spec is promised for next week. It decides what this project actually received and therefore how it will learn what it is building.

Sequence

  1. Inventory every file, link, login and named conversation offered as input: what it is, who gave it, date.
  2. Classify the set (A through F) and record it in PROJECT_CONFIG.md and STATE.md.
  3. File and freeze everything into 00-inputs/, read-only, with origin-and-date filenames. Client documents are never edited; corrections happen downstream.
  4. Fill the header block on 00-inputs/PRD.md.
  5. Audit against the fourteen dimensions, scored PRESENT / PARTIAL / ABSENT. Resist rounding up: the audit's entire value is in its ABSENTs.
  6. Convert absences into questions (grouped by who can answer) or register assumptions (where nobody can).
  7. Open the assumption register with blast radius, cheapest test, named owner and decision date per entry.
  8. Start the substitute path for classes C through F.
  9. Disclose a missing spec in STATE.md > blockers so it appears in every gate report until a signed brief exists.

The fourteen audit dimensions

1. Business goal and measure
2. Users and roles
3. Entities and vocabulary
4. Rules and constraints
5. States and lifecycle
6. Permissions
7. The current process
8. Exceptions and failure handling
9. Integrations and data boundaries
10. Non-functional expectations
11. Platform and environment
12. Success and failure scenarios
13. Out of scope
14. The client's own open questions

Dimension 14 is the strongest single quality signal a spec can carry: a document admitting no unknowns was written to persuade, not to specify. Dimension 2 is the highest-leverage absence, because role count drives artifact count for the whole project.

3.2 Phase 1: Empathize and Define PHASE 1

Owner: discovery-lead  ·  Mode: divergent, then convergent

Phase 1 is one activity, the discovery and definition call, and everything before and after it serves that call. The phase exits on a problem definition the domain authority has confirmed in writing.

spec-intake → anchor-analysis → glossary-builder → question-generation ↓ [ the D&D call ] ↓ written confirmation ← contradiction-mining ← interview-facilitation capture

Before the call

Anchor analysis reads the document forensically in three passes: as the intended reader (absorb what it wants you to conclude), as an extractor (the full claim typology, every item tagged [DOC:section]), and as a skeptic (what would a skeptical reader conclude from the same facts?). Two outputs matter most. The commitment scan captures every "must / shall / always / never / required by" sentence with its authority class (law, contract, policy, or preference) and its blast radius; the authority class is what later stops prioritization treating a 2014 policy choice as untouchable law. The silence pass asks eight questions of every described process (before, after, failure, volume, exception, authority, boundary, reversal) and records every unanswered one as a finding.

Glossary building classifies each term descriptive or rule-bearing using one test: if two people defined it differently, would the product behave differently? Rule-bearing terms get a dependency count, verification probes, and a place on the DA-verification list sorted by how many artifacts will rest on them.

Question generation derives the set from the analysis: one process question and one rules question per process, exception questions from the silences, an edge-testing question per commitment, an authority question per commitment lacking one, and a verification question per single-mention constant. Every primary question gets a 2 to 4 rung ladder written in advance (concretize, edge, actor, stake), and every question passes a lint: no yes/no primaries, no embedded answers, no hypotheticals as primaries, one question per question. Twelve well-laddered questions beat thirty flat ones; if the list exceeds the slot, cut whole questions rather than compressing all of them.

During the call

Three-column capture, [PO] [DA] [TEAM], plus side lists for solutions offered, new terms, and requirement unfolding (every question a team member asks, logged with whether it was answered). Separation at the moment of capture is the whole point: separation after the fact is reconstruction.

Technique matters more than the script. Three to five seconds of silence after an answer, then the echo probe (repeat their last few words with a question tone) before reaching for the next written rung. Every abstract answer gets "walk me through the last actual time" before the conversation moves on. Four tells are worth probing when they appear: a hesitation (uncertainty or politics), a laugh (usually a workaround), the word "just" (compression, ladder down), and "obviously" (an assumption the client cannot see).

When the conversation and the document disagree, say so out loud, neutrally, and capture both. That disagreement is among the most valuable findings the call produces. When the DA and PO debate each other, let it run while it stays concrete and capture both sides tagged; live disagreement is high-grade evidence.

The closing summary is never cut for time. Summarize back group by group, then ask verbatim: what did I get wrong, what did I not ask about, who sees this differently, what are you worried we will get wrong. Mishearings caught here cost a sentence; caught at Gate 2 they cost a re-model.

After the call

Contradiction mining runs in two stages. A model-assisted sweep with the exact framing "list every contradiction between these sources; do not resolve, reconcile or merge anything", then a human filter applying the co-satisfiability test: if both statements were true, could the same product satisfy both? Yes means phrasing, drop it. No means real, keep it. Then a deliberate under-detection pass, because models over-detect surface phrasing and under-detect deep conflicts.

Problem statements carry five fields: problem, source tag, current workaround, downstream consequence, confidence. A problem with no stated workaround queues the workaround question (people always cope somehow; an unknown coping mechanism means the problem is not yet understood). A problem with no downstream consequence is suspect and queues the stake question.

Exit criteria, enforced

Written confirmation of the problem set from the DA (a verbal yes upgrades nothing), zero owner-less contradictions, and open-questions.md converted into the Phase 2A research plan. The phase can close on an explicit, recorded risk acceptance, but never on silence.

3.3 Phase 2A: Initial Design PHASE 2A

Mode: divergent, parallel workstreams

Six workstreams, with real dependencies between them:

domain-researcher ─┐ kb-curator ────────┼─→ prompt-spec-writer ─→ prototype-engineer flow-modeler ──────┘ (needs kb-index) (needs a complete prompt) (as-is) ui-designer ── density decision + status encoding sketch ONLY data-modeler ── tentative ERD sketch flow-modeler ── tentative structure set

Research, knowledge base and as-is mapping start immediately and in parallel. Prompt writing needs the KB index to exist. Prototype generation needs a complete prompt: no prompt, no prototype, no exceptions.

The ui-designer deliberately does not build a system here. In 2A they settle the density decision and a status encoding sketch and nothing else, because a full visual system authored against an uncorrected entity model is half thrown away at Gate 1.

Controlled roughness

The tentative set (sitemap, to-be sketches, userflow sketches, ERD sketch) exists so Gate 1 has structure to reject, not just a prototype to react to. An authority who accepts a screen will often reject the data model behind it once both are visible. Three disciplines keep it useful:

  • Timeboxed, declared in STATE.md. Roughness is enforced by the clock. If a diagram is taking long enough to look finished, it has exceeded its purpose.
  • Derived, not invented. Every element carries a sources line ("derived from: as-is/invoicing rows 4-11, problem P-07"). Free-floating structure is decoration, and it survives unexamined precisely because nobody owns the claim behind it.
  • Uncertainty marked on the artifact: (inferred) labels, "?" edges, and an open-questions box in the corner of each sketch listing the two or three things the team most wants ruled on. Reviewers correct what is visibly uncertain and politely ignore what looks decided.

The prototype's fidelity asymmetry

Build properlyLeave obviously rough
Realistic content at realistic volume, including one negative, one zero and the longest plausible name Visual polish: colour, typography, shadows, icon craft
Every status the sketch entities can hold, visible somewhereAnything the scope boundary excluded
The empty state and at least one error stateAnimation and transitions
The complete happy path of the job, end to endAuth, settings, exports
Native, keyboard-operable controls with visible focusResponsive behaviour, unless a role genuinely works on a tablet
The table at the client's real row countPerfect alignment and off-scale nudging

The reason is asymmetric. A domain authority looking at five status badges will name the sixth, which is a Type B correction worth days. A domain authority looking at slightly wrong spacing will comment on the spacing, which is worth nothing at this gate. Visible roughness in the visual layer actively protects the gate's attention.

3.4 Gate 1 GATE

Attendees: domain authority + product owner  ·  Command: /gate-1

The set is reviewed together, never artifact by artifact. The prototype is introduced with its framing script out loud: disposable, built to be wrong in a useful way, tell me where it is wrong. Then the six questions, in order:

  1. Where is the as-is process factually wrong or incomplete?
  2. Which steps are missing?
  3. Which entities or relationships are wrong?
  4. Which edge cases would break this?
  5. Which terminology is wrong for this domain?
  6. What must be true that is not shown here?

The second half of the agenda is the tentative set's open-questions boxes: the team asked for specific rulings, and this is where they get ruled.

On pass: corrections classified and routed, the prototype and its prompt archived to 50-prototype/ARCHIVED/, the report filed, phase set to 2B, and reconciliation announced as the next action.

3.5 Phase 2B: Further Design PHASE 2B

Mode: convergent. The chain is strictly ordered and the order is enforced.

data-modeler permission matrix, closed status lists, lifecycle, consequences note ↓ flow-modeler to-be flows that are CORRECT, process breakdown with its cross-checks ↓ ux-architect optimize (cost tables) → structure (IA) → draw (userflows, sitemap) ↓ ui-designer the visual and interaction system ↓ hifi-spec-designer Phase 3 screens ↓ design-critic before each gate

Each arrow is a seam with a reason:

  • Optimizing before the process is correct optimizes the wrong process.
  • Drawing userflows before the structure is chosen means redrawing them.
  • Building screens before the system exists produces one-off exceptions that fork the system.

Requirements reconciliation runs first and continuously

The refined requirement document is seeded from anchor-analysis commitments, problem statements, the PRD draft, promoted register assumptions, and every Gate 1 Type B correction. Every Resolved statement passes five quality rules: testable (a reader can construct the passing and failing case), atomic (an "and" joining two obligations is two entries), solution-free (what must be true, not which widget), vocabulary-bound (every term in the glossary), and quantified where quantifiable.

Dependency links (depends-on, feeds) are wired per entry. They are what turn Type B impact analysis into a lookup instead of an archaeology dig.

The ERD is where the expensive mistakes live

The exhaustion protocol: collect candidate status values from every source (flows, the document, incumbent filter dropdowns, the current build's badges), read the list to the DA, and ask "and what else can it be?" until the answer is nothing. Then probe the gaps: what is it between X and Y, what is it while it waits, what if the user abandons it, has one ever been in two at once (if yes, there are two status fields wearing one name). Finish with the terminal check.

Cardinality by counterexample, never by rule: "has there ever been an X with two Ys?" The dangerous answers are "normally one" (model n, display for one) and "it depends" (chase the condition; it is a rule).

The temporal pass: per attribute referencing another entity's data, is the value frozen at-the-time or a live current lookup? The probe is "if the price changes tomorrow, should last month's invoice change with it?" The answer is almost always no, and getting this wrong corrupts history silently.

The consequences note is the deliverable that carries all of this to the designers, one line per rule that constrains the interface: "X is reversed, never deleted: no delete affordance anywhere; provide reverse with a reason field."

To-be design and the improvement ladder

Applied in order to every pain-marked as-is row:

RungMeaning
1. EliminateRemove the need. Ask why the step exists before how to speed it up
2. SimplifyFewer decisions, inputs, touches; same outcome
3. IntegrateKill the re-keying and the handoff seam; data moves itself
4. AutomateLast deliberately: automating a step that should have been eliminated fossilizes it

Before any elimination, the second-job check: a redundant-looking step often does control, audit trail, training or handoff signalling invisibly. Ask "who would notice if this stopped happening, and when?"

Automation honesty: a step is automatic only when its trigger and inputs are fully specified by (confirmed) entries, and every automatic step specifies detection, silence window, fallback and override. Automation does not remove the unhappy path, it relocates it.

Flow optimization: counting before proposing

Every task gets a cost table: decisions, inputs classed K (known to the system), R (retrievable) or N (genuinely new), waits, navigations, recalls, with the as-is totals beside them. Every K and R input is a defect until proven otherwise: the system is asking a person for something it already has. A to-be that does not reduce the totals is a re-platforming, and that is worth knowing before Gate 2 rather than after launch.

Friction classDefault fix
Unnecessary decisionDefault it, visibly and changeably
Unnecessary inputPrefill, derive, or look up. Show it, do not ask for it
Unnecessary waitMove it off the critical path
Unnecessary navigationBring it to the point of decision
Unnecessary recallShow it at the moment it is needed
Unnecessary rigourDowngrade per the friction budget

The last class is the one designers under-apply. Friction added out of caution costs the user on every single pass and pays off only on the rare error, and nobody audits it because it looks responsible. The friction budget sizes ceremony against the consequence data from discovery: detection latency, remediation cost, reversibility.

Information architecture

Structure derives from the entity model plus roles, never from the feature list; the feature list is a coverage check run last. Labels come from the glossary, always: a label with no glossary entry is a defect in one of the two files. The navigation model is chosen against counted inputs (primary entities, roles, role overlap, daily tasks per role, rare-but-critical tasks), and the rejected alternatives are recorded, because six months later someone will propose them again.

The structure is validated, not asserted: a tree test (eight to twelve real tasks, "where would you go to...?") and a reverse card sort, an hour with the DA and two or three users. Anything under roughly 70 percent direct success on a daily task gets reworked before it is drawn.

Prioritization closes the phase

The mandatory partition is separated first and audited against the authority classes captured back in anchor analysis: only law and contract are automatically mandatory; policy and preference compete like everything else. Only the optional partition is scored, with a method chosen and recorded (Impact/Effort, MoSCoW, RICE, WSJF, Kano, opportunity scoring, or P0 ranking), and every estimate carrying its provenance. Then the two domain rules apply, after scoring, visibly:

The two prioritization rules

1. Mandatory is not a candidate. Anything a (confirmed) DA rule or a [DOC] commitment requires is in by definition. A method that scores a legal requirement low has mis-scoped its inputs, not discovered a saving.

2. Process completeness over feature count. A half-supported process is worth less than no support: it forces users to run two systems at once. Rank in units of complete to-be flows, including their exception paths.

3.6 Gate 2 GATE

Attendees: DA + PO + at least one developer  ·  Command: /gate-2

Review order is enforced: refined requirements, ERD, breakdown, to-be pairs with their pain-index deltas, flow optimization deltas, IA, userflows, sitemap. Screens last. Screen-first review hides model contradictions, reliably.

The developer's attendance is the point, not a courtesy. Their acceptance of the ERD and their read of the breakdown are exit criteria; their structural objections are cheap now and multiples later. Hand them the two-developer-test results as a reading guide.

On pass: the prioritized list is signed in writing (marked LOCKED on fixed-bid), the prototype is confirmed archived, and Phase 3 starts from the list and the consequences note.

3.7 Phase 3: Test and Prototype PHASE 3

Owner: hifi-spec-designer, with ui-designer and design-critic

Convergent and divergent at once: each prioritized feature converges to production intent, and anything discovered that changes a requirement diverges back through the reconciler.

The state enumeration table is built before any screen. Feature by entity by status, from the ERD's closed lists rather than from memory. Each cell is a screen state to specify or an explicit "not reachable here" proved with the flow. Unproven unreachability is a missing screen. Building this table first is what stops states being discovered late and expensively.

Nine blocks per feature spec

#BlockContents
1Screen statesThe generic set (empty, loading, partial, error, success, permission denied) plus one state per ERD status value the feature's entities can hold
2Screen compositionThe three hierarchy levels named in writing before drawing; layout, density, grouping
3Dev notesBehaviour the visual cannot carry: validation rules with REQ-IDs, calculation logic, what recalculates when, server-authoritative versus client
4Interaction guidesFeedback timing, optimistic/pending/confirmed per mutation, validation timing, save model, keyboard contract, focus movement, concurrency
5Status mappingPer status: trigger, who may trigger, which transitions are irreversible, what every role sees
6Screen flowHow screens connect, exception paths drawn rather than implied
7Real contentActual copy in every state, REQ-IDs beside any rule-bearing string
8Accessibility contractElement or ARIA pattern, name source, keyboard pattern, states exposed, announcements, target sizes
9Responsive adaptationBreakpoint behaviour, dense-data strategy, target sizes by environment

Partial specs are marked DRAFT in the header. No silent partials, and no DRAFT headers left at gate time.

Prototype gravity

Sources are the refined requirements, the ERD, the flows and the IA. Never the archived prototype. If you find yourself opening ARCHIVED/, stop and say why out loud. Hi-fi that quietly refines archived screens inherits every uncorrected assumption in them.

3.8 Gate 3 GATE

Attendees: internal team  ·  Command: /gate-3

Line by line: each in-list feature's spec against its REQ-IDs. Then an independent re-run of the status-mapping-versus-ERD reconciliation, separate from the spec designer's own self-check, because that mismatch is the most common Gate 3 defect. Critique lenses 3, 4 and 7 (state completeness, accessibility, domain fidelity) are also re-run independently, because those three are where a self-critique is least reliable: they require reading the ERD rather than looking at the screen.

Passing Gate 3 unlocks scheduling client validation, not shipping.

3.9 Handoff and the md-spec loop CLOSE

Owner: handoff-manager

Handoff is a transfer of understanding, not a file drop. The measurable goal: build-time clarification requests approach zero for anything the artifact set already answers.

The what-if sweep, before the walkthrough

Per in-list feature, confirm that an artifact (not your memory) answers each of: every mutation's optimistic/pending/confirmed decision and failure behaviour; every screen state including ERD statuses; every exception path's landing state and resume point; every irreversible transition's confirmation pattern; concurrency per multi-user surface; the narrow strategy per dense table. Anything unanswered is patched that week or listed as a manifest gap. The sweep list itself is filed so the dev team can see what was checked.

The walkthrough

Live, developer present, in manifest order: requirements, ERD with the consequences note read aloud, breakdown, flow pairs, userflows and sitemap, then per-feature specs. Model before screens, same logic as Gate 2.

Two steps are easy to skip and worth protecting. Read the glossary's rule-bearing terms aloud with their definitions: the dev team will name variables and tables after these words, and those ten minutes are where the ubiquitous language either transfers or does not. Walk one feature end to end at full depth before surveying the rest: it calibrates how much answer the artifacts carry, which is what stops the team asking a person instead of reading.

Then land the routing rule explicitly: build-time questions about rules go through the reconciler path, not a designer's memory; questions about visuals go to the style guide and skills-md first. Show them the rework log so they see the mechanism working.

The handoff's own quality score

Split the question list into artifact-answered and memory-answered. Every memory-answered question is a spec gap to patch that week, not someday. A walkthrough that went well because the designer answered everything live is a walkthrough whose answers leave when the designer rolls off.

The md-spec loop

With a developer, never solo. Gather the evidence first: this project's style guide and skills-md deltas with their recorded reasons, the dev team's actual built conventions read from real code, and the build-time question log. Then adjudicate every divergence with one of three outcomes:

FindingOutcome
Diverged deliberately (reason recorded)Adopt into the shared file, or keep project-local and say why
Diverged silentlyAdopt or revert, but record either way; silent forks are the decay mechanism
Shared file asserts something nobody followsDelete it. Aspirational rules rot trust in the whole file

The adopt-versus-local test: would the rule hold on a different client with a different brand? Yes means shared. Consider at least one deletion honestly at every review; a file that only grows becomes a file nobody reads.

The three loop metrics

Recorded per project and compared across projects. All three should fall as the shared files mature. They are cheap (each is a count) and they are the difference between "we think the process works" and evidence.

Requirements changed after Gate 2 (post-G2 Type B count)Discovery or reconciliation is converging too early
