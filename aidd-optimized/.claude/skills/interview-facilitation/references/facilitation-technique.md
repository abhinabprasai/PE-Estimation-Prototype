# Facilitation Technique

Probe mechanics, bias counters, the observation protocol, and the difficult-moment playbook.

---

## Probe mechanics

| Probe | Mechanic | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Silence | 3-5 seconds after their answer, attentive posture, pen up | After every substantive answer. The follow-on sentence is disproportionately the finding |
| Echo | Repeat their last 3-6 words with a rising tone: "...fix it in the export?" | The answer ended somewhere interesting; cheaper than a question and does not redirect |
| Concretize | "Walk me through the last actual time" | Any answer in the present tense of habit ("we usually...", "the team generally...") |
| Show me | "Can you share your screen / bring one?" | Any answer describing an artifact. The artifact always disagrees with the description somewhere, and the disagreement is the finding |
| Column walk | "What is this column for? Who reads it?" | A spreadsheet or form is on screen. Every column is a requirement someone had |
| Quantify | "Is that typical or the worst case? What is a normal week's number?" | Any answer containing "a lot", "often", "sometimes", "rarely" |
| Ladder up | "Why does that matter?" (max twice) | The answer is an attribute and you need the goal it serves |
| Ladder down | "Which field exactly? What did you type?" | The answer is a summary and you need mechanics |
| Reverse | "What would have to be true for that NOT to happen?" | Testing a claimed rule's edges |
| Third party | "What would {other role} say about that?" | Politics in the room; lets the speaker voice a position that is not theirs |

## Bias counters (the facilitator's own biases)

| Bias | How it corrupts the call | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation | You hear support for the anchor document's framing and stop probing | Pass 3 skepticism carried into the room: for each key claim, ask yourself what the disconfirming answer would sound like, and check you gave it room to appear |
| Recency/vividness | The last dramatic anecdote outweighs the boring weekly pain | Quantify probe on every anecdote: "how often has that happened?" |
| Politeness inflation | Nods recorded as agreement | Only explicit statements are captured; a nod is not a tag |
| Solution attachment | A team member's pet solution shapes which problems get airtime | The UNFOLDED log makes question-asking visible; review it for skew same-day |
| Fluency trust | A confident, articulate answer feels verified | Confidence is not provenance; the tag system exists precisely because delivery quality and truth are uncorrelated in domains |
| Effort justification | After a hard-won scheduling battle, the call "must" produce a clean problem set | The deliverable is attributed statements AND contradictions; a call that produces mostly contradictions succeeded |

## Interviewee response patterns

| Pattern | What it usually means | Move |
|---|---|---|
| The idealized process | Describing the SOP, not the practice | Concretize; then "and when does it not go like that?" |
| The hero story | An exception handled brilliantly, once | Capture, then quantify: "how often?" Then find the boring version: "what about the ones that go wrong quietly?" |
| The grievance | Real pain, possibly misattributed cause | Capture the pain verbatim, tagged; probe the mechanism separately: "walk me through how that happens" |
| The expert blur | Steps compressed into "then it's just processed" | Ladder down until actors and systems are visible; "processed by whom, in what?" |
| The new-system pitch | They arrive wanting to design | SOLUTIONS-OFFERED, then steer to today: "to make sure the new thing fixes it, I need the old thing precisely" |
| The guarded answer | Short, careful, glancing at a colleague | Third-party probe, or park it and re-ask in a smaller setting; note the guardedness itself, tagged, because it maps the politics |
| "It depends" | Real branching logic | Best answer in the room: "on what? give me the two most different cases" |

## Observation protocol (contextual sessions)

Stance: master and apprentice. They are the master doing real work; you are the apprentice
watching and asking why. Not a demo (demos show the ideal path), not an interview at a desk
(memory hides workarounds).

1. Ask them to do today's actual work, not a tour. "Whatever you were going to do at 2pm, do
   that."
2. Interrupt at moments of interest, briefly: a pause ("what are you deciding right now?"), a
   switch of tool ("why did you leave the system for that?"), a paper note, a copy-paste, a
   correction.
3. Watch for the five giveaways: sticky notes and taped-up cheat sheets (missing help or rules),
   personal spreadsheets (missing entities), re-keying (missing integration), waiting (a queue
   the system does not show), and asking a colleague (missing status visibility or missing
   permission).
4. Capture interruptions and their answers with timestamps against the recording.
5. Debrief in the last ten minutes: "I saw you do X three times; is that normal? I expected Y
   to be harder; is it?"
6. Same-day write-up, same capture discipline, statements tagged [USER:role] (asserted).

Two hours with one person doing real work in a real context finds what no interview finds; run
at least one per key role when access allows.

## Difficult moments

| Moment | Playbook |
|---|---|
| DA and PO conflict escalates | Let it run while concrete; capture both, tagged. Break the loop with "can you each give me the last real example?" End without resolution: "this is exactly what this phase surfaces; I will log it with both of your positions" |
| A senior attendee dominates | Direct-address the quiet expert: "Sandra, you handle these daily; what does this look like from where you sit?" If it persists, split follow-ups into separate sessions |
| The room turns to you for the answer | Return it: "my read is only [LLM] grade; you two are the authority here. What is your instinct?" Never rule on the domain in the room |
| A statement is clearly wrong per the document | Neutral anchor: "the document says X; help me reconcile." Never "actually, the document says"; the goal is the contradiction on the table, not a point scored |
| Confidentiality flinch | "We can go off-record for this" (stop capture visibly, resume after). An off-record fact you cannot use still improves your questions |
| Time collapses | Cut whole questions by priority, announce it ("I am dropping two topics; I will send them in writing"), protect the closing summary at all costs |
| Emotional weight (fear about job changes) | Acknowledge plainly, do not promise: "I hear that; what we ship will change this workflow, and your input is what makes it change in the right direction" |

## Remote-session specifics

- Cameras on if at all possible; the four tells (hesitation, laugh, "just", "obviously") are
  half-invisible on audio alone.
- Share-version of the questions in the chat at the start; people answer better with the
  question visible.
- Screen-share requests are easier remotely; use them constantly ("can you show me that
  screen?").
- Record with consent; transcription tools produce the raw transcript, but the capture sheet
  is still built by a human, because the columns (who said it) and side lists (solutions,
  terms, unfolded) are judgment calls transcription cannot make.
- Six people on a call is a meeting, not an interview; cap discovery sessions at facilitator +
  note-taker + two or three informants, and book more sessions instead of more attendees.
